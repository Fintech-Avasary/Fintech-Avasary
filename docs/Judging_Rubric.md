# How Teams Are Judged

## Judging

Nobody advances on one person's opinion. Every entry gets two independent reviews, and each later gate has its own panel.

The first screen is deliberately light: a problem worth solving, and a team that can carry it. After that, the five criteria below do the work, and they hold from fall through spring. What changes is the evidence. Early on you can describe what you plan to build. By Final Demo Day you must show it running. A polished concept with nothing behind it won't score.

## Who Evaluates

### Three panels, three gates

Each group works at a different point in the funnel. And we keep support separate from judgment on purpose: reviewers and mentors never double as the judges who decide who advances. That way the build room stays a place you can actually ask for help.

### Reviewers — Complete

Twelve faculty reviewers scored every entry, with at least two independent reads per team, and advanced 22 teams to the build round.

**Entry gate · advanced 22**

Two independent reads per team. Late September, async.

### Lightning demo judges

3 to 6 judges watch every team's lightning demo on Friday, November 13, at 121 Tech Hub, and pick the top 10 for Final Demo Day. Scoring happens afterward.

**Top-10 cut · 3–6 judges**

One session: the lightning demo round on Friday, Nov 13.

### Final Demo Day judges

A mostly external, senior panel runs the interactive demos and selects the finalists. The deepest, most hands-on evaluation of the fall, decided on working evidence.

**Finalist cut · 4–6 judges**

Interactive demos at Final Demo Day on Wednesday, November 18.

## The Entry Gate

### Screened on the idea, not a product

Nothing is built yet at entry, so the first gate is lighter than the rubric below. Reading the one-page PDF (and the optional visuals page) and the two-minute video, reviewers look for a clear finance problem, a defined user, a solution that can realistically be built, and feasibility within the timeline.

> **Not scored at entry:** production, layout, and video quality. Two independent reviews per team set the quality floor, and 22 teams advanced into the build round.

## The Rubric

### Five criteria, weighted equally

Each criterion is worth 20% and applies in both phases. The set stays constant; what changes is how each one is read for what that phase actually tests, as the evidence bar rises from a working prototype in the fall to a refined product in the spring.

| Criterion (20% each) | Fall Build Phase | Spring Showcase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aesthetics / UI | The interface is clear and reasonably polished for a working prototype. | A polished, professional product and presentation. |
| Ease of use | A real user can complete the core task in the demo without hand-holding. | Demonstrated usability, with evidence of real use or testing. |
| Finance understanding | Sound finance and domain reasoning: a real problem, credible assumptions and data. | Depth of finance and domain reasoning, and validated claims. |
| AI implementation | Effective, appropriate, and disclosed use of AI and vibe-coding to build the solution. | Sophisticated, responsible AI use, clearly disclosed. |
| Creativity / uniqueness | Original problem framing or approach, differentiated from off-the-shelf tools. | Originality, venture potential, and differentiation. |
